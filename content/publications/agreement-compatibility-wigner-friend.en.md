This work revisits Wigner's Friend paradox as a problem of Bayesian inference. The authors examine compatibility and agreement between the descriptions assigned by different agents and argue that, from this perspective, the original formulation does not require a contradiction between Wigner's and his Friend's descriptions. The work also investigates ways of reconciling these descriptions, including a strategy referred to as the 'benefit of the doubt', in both classical and quantum settings.
