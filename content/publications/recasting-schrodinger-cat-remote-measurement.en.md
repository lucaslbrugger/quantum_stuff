This work proposes a reinterpretation of Schrödinger's cat thought experiment by viewing quantum theory as a generalization of classical probability within a subjective Bayesian framework. The experiment is treated as a remote measurement problem, focusing on how the beliefs of two spatially separated agents who share a quantum state are updated after local measurements. The approach also aims to provide an educational perspective on foundational aspects of quantum theory.
